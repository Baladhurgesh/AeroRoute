# AeroRoute project facts

## Data acquisition
- Use Python 3.11+ and curl; raw acquisition uses only the Python standard library. Developed and tested with Python 3.14.3 on macOS.
- The selected dataset is BTS Marketing Carrier On-Time Performance, January 2020 through December 2025, not the Reporting Carrier table.
- All 72 original monthly archives are downloaded and passed full offline checksum/ZIP/CSV verification: 40,910,253 records and 2,163,774,074 compressed bytes. An idempotent rerun skipped all 72 valid archives without downloading them again.
- Source archive directory: https://transtats.bts.gov/PREZIP/.
- Preserve original monthly ZIPs under `data/raw/bts_marketing/{year}/`; data is excluded from Git. Do not extract all CSVs to disk.
- `manifest.json` in the data directory records source metadata, SHA-256, original CSV headers, dates, row counts, and cancellation/diversion counts. Local hashes are not BTS-published signatures.
- Run only one downloader per output directory. Reruns skip checksum-validated completed files and restart incomplete monthly transfers. Conflicting completed files are not overwritten.
- Real BTS headers can include `Operating_Airline ` with trailing whitespace and an empty trailing column. Validation normalizes names for presence checks but preserves original records and headers.
- Required headers include both flight numbers, cancellation code, actual clock times, diversion landing count, and airport codes/IDs/wheels-on/wheels-off for all five diversion stops. Header presence does not imply a non-null value on every flight.

## Constraints for later preprocessing
- Keep cancellations and diversions even when ordinary arrival fields are null. Use 2020–2024 for historical fitting and keep 2025 outcomes isolated for evaluation.
- Obtain an airport-to-IANA-timezone lookup before reconstructing timestamps. Handle local dates, midnight rollover, DST, and missing/ambiguous timezone mappings explicitly.
- Scheduled arrival UTC can be derived from dated scheduled departure UTC plus CRSElapsedTime, with local arrival clock fields used for cross-checks.
- Div1Airport is not necessarily the final diversion airport. Inspect DivAirportLandings and all available diversion stops.
- DivActualElapsedTime and DivArrDelay apply to diverted flights reaching their scheduled destination. If a stranded diversion's timeline cannot be reconstructed reliably, terminate with a documented unresolved-timing reason; do not invent a timestamp.
- Weekday and departure-time buckets can be derived from FlightDate and CRSDepTime. Delay-cause fields are retained in raw archives but are not required for empirical outcome sampling.

## Episode record contract
- `aeroroute.domain.records` (also exported through legacy `aeroroute.records`) exports five primary frozen dataclasses: TripRequest, FlightOption, FlightOutcome, DecisionStep, and EpisodeRecord, plus typed settings/provenance/airport/diversion/terminal helpers. Construction validates types and invariants; collections are immutable tuples.
- `start_at` means the traveler is present at the origin, not already boarding-ready. The initial decision is at start_at. All record timestamps must already be dated UTC; local-clock reconstruction stays in preprocessing.
- For a resolved normal or diverted arrival, `next_decision_at == actual_arrival_at`. Arrival is before the connection buffer. The next step's `decision_at` is this timestamp; `ready_to_board_at` is decision_at plus exactly one connection buffer.
- Settings separately define initial boarding buffer (default 60 minutes), connection buffer (45), and cancellation boarding buffer (0). These are configurable modeling assumptions, not BTS-observed quantities.
- For cancellation, `next_decision_at` is the modeled recovery/notification time, not an arrival timestamp. Apply only cancellation_boarding_buffer_minutes afterward; do not apply the initial or connection buffer again. The environment must record the recovery policy/version in timing_method and its parameters in EpisodeProvenance.transition_parameters; record validation does not invent this time or enforce an unimplemented recovery formula.
- Scheduled departure must meet readiness at selection. Every known actual departure must also meet readiness; impossible early historical departures are rejected, not clamped. The future environment must explicitly decide how to handle unboardable sampled outcomes rather than silently resampling away their risk.
- `episode_end_at` is derived from start_at plus max_duration_minutes. arrival_deadline is a grading target, not a stopping rule. Arrival exactly at the hard cutoff is allowed; new selections at the cutoff and observed outcome events after it are not.
- If a cutoff interrupts a flight, censor unobserved completion fields and use unresolved_reason=`episode_cutoff` with termination_reason=`time_limit`. OutcomeStatus.UNRESOLVED covers unclassified/in-flight outcomes; a known diversion may retain its status and observed stops while its final airport/time remain null. A timeout while waiting at a known airport may advance the terminal clock to the cutoff.
- `TerminalState.last_known_at` is the last known trace timestamp, not necessarily the arrival time at TerminalState.current_airport. Unknown next_decision_at stays null; no further action is allowed after an unresolved outcome.
- Flight arrival delay is defined only if reached_scheduled_destination is true and actual_arrival_at is known. Trip lateness is separately derived against the requested trip destination/deadline. A diversion to the trip destination can complete the trip even if it did not reach that leg's scheduled destination.
- Do not store a success flag or reward in the trace. Arrival time, lateness, cancellation counts, and diversion counts are derived. Invalid actions raise validation errors; no invalid_action or immediate-cancellation terminal behavior has been implemented.
- Store full details only for the selected flight, with compact offered-flight IDs and a shared immutable schedule reference. Selected instances cannot be attempted twice. No-feasible-flights claims and authenticity of offered IDs still require reconstructing the search against that schedule and recorded settings; these records provide consistency checks, not proof of authenticity.

## Provenance and serialization
- SourceRecordRef.source_row_id is the 1-based ordinal of the original CSV data record, excluding the header, within csv_member of the archive identified by source_sha256. It is not a filtered DataFrame index or a physical text-line number. Preserve it during ingestion.
- dataset_version/outcome_dataset_version are lowercase SHA-256 identifiers of a frozen dataset inventory snapshot; freeze the inventory before simulation rather than hashing the changing download-progress manifest on each step. Snapshot materialization belongs to the preprocessing phase.
- ScheduleRef stores a snapshot name and content SHA-256. HistoricalSampleRef separately stores its source, original flight date, pool ID/size/fallback level, and matching_pool_sha256 identifying the ordered pool membership. A source row index or seed without these references is insufficient for reproducible sampling.
- EpisodeProvenance stores simulator/sampler versions, seed, schedule identity, outcome dataset identity, and immutable string key/value tuples for sampler and transition parameters. Referenced snapshot/pool objects must be retained and checked by the later data-access layer.
- Use `EpisodeRecord.to_dict()/from_dict()` and `to_json()/from_json()` for portable records. JSON uses UTC ISO-8601 strings, enum strings, arrays, and explicit nulls; decoding restores typed tuples/dates/enums and reruns validation. Unsupported schema versions, unknown/duplicate fields, and coerced scalar types are rejected.
- Test records alone: `uv run python -m unittest discover -s tests -p test_records.py -v`. Run all tests with the command above.

## Service catalog and outcome providers
- `aeroroute.storage.dataset.DatasetStore` verifies a normalized snapshot and its separately hashed source inventories. `SourceRecordRef.dataset_version` identifies an inventory, not the normalized snapshot digest. Selected fitting inventories can cover a strict subset of historical years.
- Build inspection artifacts with `uv run python -m aeroroute.cli.build_data_access --snapshot /absolute/path/to/dataset_manifest.json --stage inspect`. The default output is project-anchored `data/processed/access/`. `--dry-run` verifies inputs without writes; `--verify-only --artifact /absolute/path/to/artifact` verifies lookup/services or complete pilot artifacts without rebuilding.
- `source_record_id` remains the archive/member/original-CSV-ordinal identity. `service_instance_id` is a schedule-only, versioned identity; `FlightOption.flight_id` uses that service identity. Supporting source records are retained in the catalog; `schedule_source` is its primary schedule reference.
- The initial `exact-operating-service-v1` resolution policy groups matching dated operating departures, checks schedule conflicts and marketing/original-service aliases, and rejects ambiguous groups. `Duplicate=Y` is never a generic deletion instruction. A nonempty cited `--review-reference` records external pilot review; it is not automatically supplied by the builder. Schedule/evidence conflicts still prevent readiness after review. New authority/override rules require a separate reviewed policy, not an arbitrary source preference.
- The catalog's retained source/stop lookup maps immutable record IDs to artifact-hash-bound Parquet file, row group, and within-group row offset. Physical offsets never replace original CSV ordinals. Runtime lookup uses projected row-group reads with byte-bounded LRU and file-handle limits; queries do not build indexes or scan monthly Parquet files.
- `ScheduleStore.departures` uses inclusive readiness and exclusive departure upper bounds. It never filters by observed outcomes or by scheduled arrival versus deadline. Queries are complete relative to the published schedule-eligibility policy, not proof of complete real-world schedule coverage. Unknown timezone/period coverage raises rather than returning an empty offer set.
- Departure queries return bounded pages (default 1,000; maximum 10,000). Follow `next_cursor` until `complete` is true; cursors bind the snapshot, airport, window, and attempted service set. Attempting a service excludes all its aliases. Schedule shards are sized independently of airport/date lookup keys.
- Pools weight resolved historical services uniformly, not supporting-row multiplicity. A retained pool binds ordered service/source membership, fitting inventory, matching hierarchy, and resolution policy. `matching_pool_size` counts services. Source evidence is never independently mixed, re-dated, filtered by traveler readiness, or resampled to avoid cancellations/diversions/unresolved observations.
- Matching defaults are provisional: six-hour local scheduled-departure buckets, 30-service support target, and progressively relaxed route-preserving criteria. `--retain-time-of-day` relaxes quarter before time bucket. `PreparedPools.validation_report(service_ids)` reports request-weighted fallback/carrier/time-of-day/low-support/unsupported rates and rejects 2025+ or fitting-year requests. Coverage reports on held-out schedule attributes are not permission to tune on held-out outcomes.
- `Scenario` binds a pool set and schedule. Seed, scenario ID, selected service ID, membership identity, and sampler version generate the draw using SHA-256 rejection sampling. Episode labels and step indices are trace-only. The same frozen scenario/service gives the same evidence across policies and query order.
- Exact replay has separately prepared singleton membership mappings and returns the held-out departure's authoritative evidence, including incomplete observations. It never falls back to fitting pools. Schedule identity conflicts or conflicting supporting observations block readiness; missing observation fields alone do not.
- After review, the synthetic/live-path entry point is `uv run python -m aeroroute.cli.build_data_access --snapshot /absolute/path/to/dataset_manifest.json --stage pilot --review-reference REVIEW_ID --schedule-periods 2025-01 --fitting-years 2020`. It queries a held-out departure, reproduces a fitting-only draw at two trace steps, retrieves replay evidence, retains scenario/receipts, and measures cold-application-cache and warm lookups. Use `--service-id` to choose a particular departure. The default first held-out departure is not silently replaced if its pool is unsupported.
- Providers return historical evidence, not traveler transitions. The separate `simulation` package now implements the explicitly approved transition policy described below. Replay claims remain historical replay under explicit traveler/recovery assumptions, not proof of seat availability or real passenger rebooking feasibility.
- Data-access tests: `uv run python -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_data_access*.py' -v`; additionally run `test_service_resolution.py`, `test_schedule.py`, and `test_sampling.py`, or the full project suite above.

## Simulation and verification MVP
- `simulation.environment.FlightEnvironment` implements `reset(request, episode_id=...)` and `step(service_instance_id)`. Observations contain only the current traveler state and schedule options; raw future outcome evidence stays in the provider. Step results contain the observed/censored outcome, reward, and terminated/truncated indicators. Invalid/unoffered actions fail without changing episode state.
- New simulator traces use episode schema version 2; version 1 remains decodable. Version 2 adds `OutcomeStatus.MISSED_BOARDING` without changing the serialized field layout. A missed service leaves the traveler at the origin with no boarded-flight departure/arrival; its underlying early departure remains in referenced historical evidence. Version-1 episodes reject this new status.
- Approved `whole-timeline-recovery-v1`: empirical mode shifts every supported historical event by selected scheduled departure minus historical scheduled departure. It preserves observed intervals, not necessarily delay relative to a different scheduled arrival. Replay keeps original timestamps. Every assumption is bound in episode provenance.
- Cancellation notification/recovery is modeled at scheduled departure plus configurable recovery minutes (default 60), then the existing cancellation boarding buffer applies. A cancellation with reported departure evidence terminates explicitly as unsupported/uncertain rather than inventing a return-to-gate timeline.
- If a known actual departure precedes readiness, missed boarding is observed at scheduled departure. The traveler may recover, the service cannot be retried, and `missed_boarding_buffer_minutes` (default 0) is explicitly recorded in transition parameters. Initial and connection buffers are not reapplied to missed/cancelled services.
- Ordinary/resolved diverted arrivals set next decision equal to gate arrival before the connection buffer. Unresolved diversion gate timing terminates explicitly. A resolved arrival time without departure evidence does not fabricate a fully resolved flown transition. Unsupported pool/coverage accesses remain infrastructure errors, not no-feasible-flights claims or redraws.
- Cutoffs censor future completion and stop events. A known diversion stop departure can be retained even if its landing time is missing. Censored outcomes terminate with `time_limit`/`episode_cutoff`; arrival exactly at the cutoff is allowed. Decision limits also truncate. Arrival deadlines are metrics, not stopping rules.
- Reward is zero on nonterminal steps. The terminal reward is `reliability-rubric-v1`: destination reached 60, deadline met 25, no cancellation/diversion/missed boarding 10, and known elapsed time within the episode limit 5. Each component scores 0 or 1, and the total is their weighted sum divided by 100. A clean on-time arrival scores 1. The same function scores the environment and the verifier.
- `verification.verifier.EpisodeVerifier` takes trusted schedule/provider/settings/policy handles and a trusted TripRequest. It revalidates serialization, reconstructs complete offered sets, reproduces source sampling/replay, reapplies the pure versioned transition specification, and checks terminal state/reason. It does not execute the environment or trust stored success/reward flags. Shared transition rules are not an independently implemented second physics model.
- Verifier output includes validity, destination/deadline metrics, arrival/lateness, elapsed time, last-known elapsed time, decisions, cancellations, observed diversions, missed boardings, the four named rubric components, and `terminal_reward`. Report version is `reliability-rubric-v1`. Elapsed time remains null for unresolved timing, except a known hard cutoff supplies the episode duration. Invalid traces raise `VerificationError` rather than receiving a score.
- Run a baseline episode with `uv run python -m aeroroute.cli.simulate --snapshot /path/to/dataset_manifest.json --pilot /path/to/access/pilots/ID --mode replay` (or `empirical`). Use `--request` for a serialized TripRequest; otherwise a schedule-only smoke request is derived from the pilot departure. The deterministic baseline prefers direct destination flights, then earliest scheduled arrival. It does not inspect realized outcomes when choosing actions.
- Episode artifacts contain `experiment.json`, `episode.json`, `verification.json`, and a manifest under `data/processed/episodes/`. Recheck with the same snapshot/pilot arguments plus `--verify-episode /path/to/episode/artifact`. No rewards/success summaries were added to EpisodeRecord itself.
- New tests: `test_environment.py`, `test_verifier.py`, `test_simulation_integration.py`, and `test_storage_compact.py`; run the full project command above. Simulation/verification imports do not depend on normalization or CLI modules.

## Gymnasium adapter and agent examples
- Install/run the optional pinned RL dependencies with `uv sync --extra rl` and `uv run --extra rl ...` (Gymnasium 1.2.0, NumPy 2.3.3). Core/domain/simulation package imports do not import Gymnasium; import `simulation.gymnasium_env.FlightGymEnv` explicitly.
- `FlightGymEnv(environment, request, max_flights=1000, episode_id="episode")` wraps a prepared `FlightEnvironment`. `reset(seed=..., options={"request": request, "episode_id": "..."})` supports per-episode overrides without changing constructor defaults. The adapter borrows schedule/provider handles: `close()` does not close them; their owning context must do so. Do not reset or step the borrowed core separately while an adapter episode is active.
- Observations are a Dict: `flights` float64 `(max_flights, 5)` with columns origin airport ID, destination airport ID, scheduled departure minutes from request start, scheduled arrival minutes from start, and is-trip-destination; `state` float64 `(8,)` with current airport ID (0 if unknown), trip destination airport ID, decision minutes (0 if unknown), readiness minutes (0 if unknown), deadline minutes, hard duration minutes, decision count, and core-done indicator; `time_known` int8 `(2,)` disambiguates decision/readiness nulls; `action_mask` int8 `(max_flights + 1,)` identifies legal slots. No future outcome evidence or historical source references are encoded.
- Flight slots preserve complete schedule offer order; `info["flight_ids"]` maps active rows to public schedule IDs. Padded/invalid actions raise without advancing the episode. Capacity overflow raises instead of dropping flights; increase `max_flights` for the declared experiment. Action-space sampling must pass the current mask: `env.action_space.sample(mask=observation["action_mask"])`. Generic unmasked policies/checkers can choose illegal slots.
- Slot `max_flights` is a terminal acknowledgement only when reset already discovers no feasible flights or a hard limit. This allows the normal Gym loop to receive terminated/truncated on its first step without fabricating a flight, provider draw, or DecisionStep. After ordinary termination or acknowledgement all action-mask values are zero and further steps require reset. Time/decision limits truncate; other core terminal reasons terminate.
- Gymnasium reset seeds control adapter/action-space randomness only, not the frozen provider scenario. `info` records this scope, provider mode, and empirical scenario ID/seed. To compare outcome scenarios, prepare/select explicitly different scenario-bound providers; do not treat repeated reset seeds as new historical outcome draws.
- `simulation.agents.RandomAgent(seed=...)` samples legal actions using its own RNG. `EarliestArrivalAgent()` prefers direct destination, then earliest scheduled arrival/departure, then original offer order. `make_agent("random" | "earliest-arrival", seed=...)` constructs either. They consume only encoded observations, not `info` or provider handles. Agent RNG and adapter RNG seeds are separate; instantiate a new seeded agent to reproduce its action sequence.
- `simulation.agents.run_episode(env, agent, seed=..., options=...)` returns frozen `RolloutResult(record, total_reward, terminated, truncated)` and does not autoreset after termination. The caller verifies the record with the existing trusted `EpisodeVerifier`; the runner does not itself certify traces. Total reward is the reliability rubric, including the acknowledgement step for an episode that is already finished at reset.
- Runnable prepared-artifact example: `uv run --extra rl python -m aeroroute.cli.simulate --snapshot /path/to/dataset_manifest.json --pilot /path/to/access/pilots/ID --mode replay --interface gymnasium --agent earliest-arrival`. For the random example use `--agent random --agent-seed 42`; empirical draws use `--mode empirical` with the pilot's retained scenario. `--request` selects a serialized TripRequest and `--max-flights` sets fixed action capacity. These examples require completed prepared artifacts; they do not rebuild data.
- The CLI verifies every Gymnasium rollout before publishing the existing episode/experiment/report bundle, checks total reward against verifier output, and records adapter/agent configuration outside EpisodeRecord. Existing `--verify-episode` works without importing optional RL dependencies. Tests exercise both agents and both provider modes using temporary synthetic artifacts, including deterministic repeat publication and re-verification; this is not a real-BTS evaluation or trained-policy benchmark.
- Focused checks: `uv run --extra rl python -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_gymnasium_env.py' -v`, similarly `test_agents.py` and `test_simulation_integration.py`. Full verification passed 222 tests; simulating unavailable Gymnasium/NumPy passed with 40 optional tests skipped. `uv lock --check` and `git diff --check` also passed. The Gymnasium checker test supplies legal masked samples and emits advisory warnings for unbounded numeric Box spaces; observation-space sampling itself is finite and covered by a regression test.

## Compact post-processing storage
- Lookup version 2 stores archive metadata once and uses binary SHA-256 keys plus compact per-record locators. Service builder version 2 stores compressed canonical schedule attributes once per service and retains compact support mappings; candidate payloads and alias indexes used during construction are temporary. Readers continue accepting version-1 lookup/catalog artifacts; old artifacts are never migrated or overwritten.
- Source IDs, CSV ordinals, schedule attributes, service weighting, and empirical/replay separation are unchanged by physical compression. Physical/catalog hashes may change, so scenarios remain pinned to their exact artifact set.
- Lookup version 3 stores a verified row-group ordinal range per contiguous flight group, plus a sparse diversion-stop index. Reordered groups keep explicit locators. Service builder version 3 writes one typed, dictionary-encoded monthly projection and a separate support mapping; public service identities stay SHA-256 digests and internal keys are dense integers. Schedule builder version 2 is a covering index for the requested periods. Pool builder version 2 publishes frozen definitions and expands membership lazily into a sidecar cache that is outside the scenario identity. Readers still accept lookup/service versions 1–2 and schedule/pool version 1. Normalized partitions are not rewritten, and old access artifacts are not deleted by the new builder.
- The data-access CLI requires 3 GiB free before building and stops before a month if less than 1.5 GiB remains. Up to four months are resolved at once, then merged in calendar order so service keys stay dense and ordered. Service identities are stored as 32-byte keys; schedule attributes stay in the normalized Parquet. This is scratch space, not a copy of the 72-month flights. No existing data is automatically deleted to make room.
